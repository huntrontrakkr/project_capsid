Crossing cost: pairing nodes by reflection

Cells A and B share a face. Mirror cell A across the line of that face and it lands exactly on cell B, so every node of A's shell lands on one node of B's shell. Those six pairs are compared: a pair is alike when both nodes are filled or both are empty. Tap any node to fill or empty it.

Each node sits at a corner of the inner ring, pointing at a corner of the cell (a beacon position). Faces are numbered clockwise from the top, 0 to 5, and node k points at the corner between faces k and k+1. Across shared face d, node d+k of A pairs with node d+2−k of B. Swapping A and B gives the same pairs, so the cost is the same in both directions.
